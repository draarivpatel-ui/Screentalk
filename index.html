You can't order your parents a colonoscopy.
But you can start the conversation.

Screening tests can find some cancers early, before symptoms, when they are easier to treat. ScreenTalk turns the American Cancer Society (ACS) guidelines into a simple plan for the people you love, then helps you bring it up.

In 2022, only 61% of U.S. adults ages 45 to 75 were up to date with colorectal cancer screening, so about 4 in 10 were behind (CDC).

    2 · Start the talk

    Health talks are awkward. Pick a person and a style, then say it out loud or send it.

    Teens often write for a parent who reads a different language. This sets the language of the message and of the printed checkup sheet; the rest of the app stays as it is.

    Tips that make it easier
    • Pick a calm moment, not right after bad news or during an argument.
    • Lead with care: “I want you around for a long time.”
    • Offer one concrete thing: find the clinic number, sit with them while they call, or set a reminder.
    • If they say no, that is okay. You planted the idea. Try again in a few weeks.

    Rehearse the talk

    Practice with a parent who pushes back: “I feel fine,” “too expensive,” “scared of a colonoscopy,” “no family history,” “no time.” Every coaching tip is backed by ACS, NCI, or CDC. In English or Spanish.
