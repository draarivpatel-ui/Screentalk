# Spanish glossary (Glosario en español)

ScreenTalk's Spanish is neutral Latin American Spanish. Medical terms follow the CDC and NCI Spanish pages listed below (all read on October 10, 2026). `tests/i18n.test.js` fails if the Spanish text uses a different variant of these terms.

**Status: pending review by a native speaker.** The Spanish was written for this project and checked against the pages below, but nobody who grew up speaking Spanish has reviewed it yet. Things a reviewer should look at first: the tone of the "Start the talk" messages (they use *tú* with a parent; some families use *usted*), the game and habit wording, and whether "proveedor de atención médica" reads naturally or "médico" is better in places.

## Which links change in Spanish

- **Guideline text stays ACS.** The plan summarizes the English ACS guidelines (revised May 27, 2026), so each topic keeps its ACS link, labelled "(en inglés)". Each topic also gets an "En español: CDC" link. CDC follows the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force, so a few ages differ (for example, CDC says mammograms every 2 years from 40; ACS says yearly from 45 with the option at 40). The plan note says this in Spanish.
- **Myth or Fact:** the 9 NCI cards link the NCI Spanish myths page. The NCI Spanish page gives "cerca de 38 por ciento" for lifetime cancer diagnosis while the English page gives 39%, so the Spanish card says "cerca de 4 de cada 10 personas", which matches both. The 3 ACS cards keep the English ACS page, marked "(en inglés)".
- **Get help:** the NCI Cancer Information Service (Spanish contact page, Spanish and English, Mon to Fri 9 am to 9 pm ET), the ACS Spanish "Cómo hacerse las pruebas de detección" page, the CDC Spanish NBCCEDP provider list, the CDC Spanish HPV vaccine page, and the NCI quitline 1-877-448-7848 (Spanish and English), all from the NCI and CDC Spanish pages.
